Bulldog: an office of teams on Corbie, and how to write one for a person.

Bulldog serves one office: people and workers in teams, asked things through bridges, with decisions that wait for the right yes. The office is a folder of files, and the files are the office: what runs is what is in them, and nothing about it is kept anywhere else. A worker is a Corbie machine, sent to Corbie as it is written.

A person has asked you to write an office, or to change one: a team, a worker, a rule, a routine or a bridge.

1. Read the page office with the manual tool before you write a file. It says how an office is written, with a whole worker, rule and bridge. Then read Corbie's pages for what you write: "definitions", "scripts", "permissions", the page of the machine type ("agent", "flow" or "statechart") and the page of each plugin a script calls. The manual gives every field and every call, so never guess one.
2. Give each job only the permissions it needs, in its allow list, and tell the person what you allowed.
3. Read the office with the bulldog tool's check, naming its folder. It reads the folder as the server would, starting nothing and writing nothing, and says each team and worker it found and every problem, with its file. Mend each problem and check again, until there are none.
4. The people of the office, how each is reached, who decides a change to the office and a bridge's in machine are the person's to write. Say what you would write there, and let them write it.
5. What you write in the folder is an edit by hand, and the person's to commit; a served office reads it again as soon as a file changes. Tell the person what you changed, what check said, and that `corbie bulldog <folder>` serves the office.

`corbie bulldog init <folder>` makes a folder an office: an empty one is given the example, a house with its workers, a rule and a routine, which is a good place to start.
