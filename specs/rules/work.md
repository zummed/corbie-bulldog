# Work: rules

The detailed rules behind the [Work overview](../work.md). Requirements are `R-work-nn`, constraints `C-work-nn`. Every test runs a whole Corbie in the test's process with a stand-in model that answers as it is told, and a clock the test moves, and steps the work until it is still. "The house" and "the work office" are the two offices of the README: the house is the top team, Sam and Kit with the steward, the fixer, sparks, the clerk, bins and the builder, the steward its lead, with the garden inside it, Sam with the gardener its lead; the work office is Al and the bot at the top, the bot its lead, with platform (no people), payments (Lee and Priya, the ledger its lead, a disclosure check) and support (Dana, its lead) inside it. Platform's decisions reach Al, through the office's `everything`.

## What starts a run

**R-work-01** A request opened to a team whose lead is a worker starts that worker on it, in a run of kind `work`, once the team's heed lets it through where one weighs it (R-work-49); a request asked at a place on a bridge is to the team that place names, the office where it names none.
*Accept:* in the house, Kit says "the kitchen socket sparked" in the built-in chat. One run of the steward is started; its labels are `bulldog` with the office's identity, `kind=work`, `team=office` and `request` with the new request's number, and its input `said` is Kit's words. In the work office, Dana writes in #help, a place that names no team: a run of the bot starts, with `team=office`. A place of the Slack bridge whose settings name payments: what Priya says there starts a run of the ledger, with `team` naming payments.

**R-work-02** A request to a team whose lead is a person starts no run, and waits on that person.
*Accept:* in the work office, the bot calls `ask_team` naming support. No run is started for the sub-request, which waits on Dana. Dana answers it on the screens, and her answer is the bot's tool result.

**R-work-03** Something said to the team in a request's thread, a reply, an answer that came after the run that asked for it ended, or a decider's refusal of an answer the team would have given, starts the lead on the request again when no run of the request is going, its lead's or its heed's, with what was said as `said`, once the team's heed lets it through where one weighs it (R-work-49).
*Accept:* the steward answers Kit, and the request is answered. Kit replies "it sparked again". A second run of the steward starts on the same request; its `said` is Kit's reply and its context holds the whole thread, the steward's first answer included.

**R-work-04** A request has at most one run going at a time, its lead's or its heed's (C-work-60); what is said in it while a run goes is given to the next run, which starts as soon as that run ends.
*Accept:* the stand-in holds the steward's run on a tool. Kit says two more things in the request meanwhile, and no second run starts. When the stand-in lets the first run end, exactly one run starts, and its `said` holds both things, in the order they were said.

**R-work-05** The office has at most `runs:` runs working at once, from the top team's file, 3 if it gives none. They are the runs of workers on requests; a heed's run (R-work-50), and a run another area asks for, to decide or for a bridge, do not count. A run with a call waiting on a person, another team or a decision is not working and does not count. A request ready to start waits its turn, oldest first.
*Accept:* with `runs: 2` and the stand-in holding every run, four requests are opened one after another. Two runs start. The first of them calls `ask_person`, and the third request's run starts. The second run ends, and the fourth starts. With no `runs:` written, a fourth request waits while three runs work, and starts when one ends.

**C-work-01** A worker may have runs going on several requests at once; what is one at a time is the request. A run that waits on a person may wait for days, and a team asked back by a team it asked would otherwise never be served. *Test:* `work_one_run_per_request_not_per_worker`

**C-work-02** A worker the office reports as having a problem is given no run. Asking a team whose lead cannot run is refused where it is asked, saying why (C-requests-01); a request already open when its lead stops is answered, the asker told that the team cannot answer now and the team's people told the problem. A definition Corbie refuses cannot run, and a request must not sit as working with nothing that could answer it. *Test:* `work_broken_lead_answers_with_its_problem`

**C-work-03** No run is given the conversation of an earlier run; each is given the thread. A conversation is what was read, and the thread is the one account of a request every run starts from. *Test:* `work_no_conversation_carried_between_runs`

**C-work-04** A request is given at most 20 runs in a row with nothing said in it by a person between them; a decider's answer, a person's included, is not something said in it, and a heed's run is not among them. The next is not started: the request is answered as stopped at the limit, or, where the last run was started by a refused answer, the asker is told that the team did not disclose its answer, and who refused it (R-work-46); a person saying something in it starts it again and begins the count again. Workers can start each other for ever, a check can refuse for ever, and a limit that only a person resets never leaves a request that nobody can start. *Test:* `work_runs_in_a_row_reset_by_a_person`

**R-work-49** At a channel place, any place on a bridge but its direct place, a team that names a heed (R-office-36) has it weigh what is new to the lead before the lead starts, when everything new is a message, the asking or more said, by a person or a guest, that came in through the request's own bridge at the request's own place, and is not the asking of a request whose origin opened a source thing, such as an issue, which the bridge names as `about` (R-bridges-10). Anything else new, alongside it or alone, starts the lead on all of it with no heed: what is said on the screens, the chat among them, or in a direct message, or through a direct message into the request; a routine's request and a sub-request; a reply to what was put to someone, a late answer and a decider's refusal (C-work-56). What is said while the lead's run goes is weighed when that run ends. A heed with a problem is not asked, and the lead starts as if there were none.
*Accept:* the work office names `heed: ./for-us.js`, a bare script that acts on what names `@bot` and notes the rest with "Not for the bot.". Dana says "Raj, I'll look at it" in #help: the heed's run notes it, the bot is not started, and the request is answered and Noted, with the heed's entry by `for-us.js` to the office, holding its note, what was read of the office. She says "@bot what do you think?" in the thread: the heed acts, and the bot starts with it alone as `said`. Al says more on the screens: the bot starts with no heed. Dana in a direct message to the bot, listed: the bot starts with no heed. Platform names the same heed: Al's GitLab issue opened in platform/api, with its `about`, starts ops with no heed, and his "thanks" in it later is weighed and noted.

**R-work-50** A team's heed is a run of kind `heed`, of the team's, noted before its start is sent and taken as a lead's run is, and never waited on. It is its rule as written, a bare script or a definition, in the team's folder or one Bulldog ships (R-work-47), started with three inputs and no `context`: `said`, each message it weighs on its own line with when it was said and who said it, in the office's zone; `request`, the request as its lead's run is given it (R-work-08), with those messages as `new`; and `settings`, every key the team file writes beside the rule, as written, and an empty mapping for a heed written as a path alone (C-work-58). Its labels are `bulldog`, `kind=heed`, the request's `team` and `request` (C-work-59). Bulldog keeps no time limit of its own on a heed: its time is its rule's, Corbie's own for a bare script, and a definition's as it is written. A heed's run counts toward neither `runs:` nor the runs in a row.
*Accept:* the office names `heed: { rule: ./for-us.js, threshold: 0.6, model: jev }`. Dana says "lovely weather" in #help: the heed's run is of kind `heed`, labelled with the office and the request, and its input holds `settings` `{ model: jev, threshold: 0.6 }`, one message in `request`'s `new`, `said` naming Dana with the time, and no `context`. Written as `heed: ./for-us.js`, its `settings` is `{}`.

**R-work-51** A heed answers `{ says: act | note, note }`, as its output or as text that holds it. On `note`, its word is written on each message it weighed (R-requests-47), and the request is dealt with up to them, so its lead does not start on them; a write that fails is said to the team's people, and the heed's ending is taken again later. On `act`, the lead starts on everything new at that moment, what was said while the heed weighed among it. A heed that fails, runs out of time, is cancelled or lost, or answers anything else, counts as act (C-work-57). A heed whose request was stopped lets nothing through. Each heed's run is kept with its request, with what it said and its note.
*Accept:* the office's heed notes "Raj, I'll look at it" with "Not for the bot.": its run is kept as done, `note: Not for the bot.`, and the request holds the heed's entry on the message. Its heed is an agent that is held while it weighs "@bot first", and Dana says "and second" meanwhile; the heed acts, and the bot starts once, given both.

**C-work-56** A reply to what the lead put to someone, a late answer and a decider's refusal are never weighed, nor is anything said on the screens or in a direct message: each starts the lead with no heed. The lead asked for the first three itself, and a heed that noted them would leave its question answered to nobody; what is said on the screens or directly is said to the team. *Test:* `work_an_answer_to_the_lead_is_never_heeded`, which holds that Dana's answer in #help to the bot's question, after the bot's run ended, starts the bot with it as `said` and no second heed.

**C-work-57** A heed that fails, runs out of time, is cancelled or lost, or answers neither act nor note, counts as act: the lead starts on everything new, and the team's people are told in one entry what Corbie said, or what it answered, and that the lead was started, as "for-us.js failed: …. bot was started."; the asker is told nothing (C-work-11). A heed failing closed would silence the team whenever its model is away, and nobody would know. *Test:* `work_a_heed_that_fails_starts_the_lead`

**C-work-58** A heed is given its settings whole and as written, an empty mapping when it has none, and no `context`; Bulldog reads none of them. A rule knows its own settings and their defaults, and Bulldog reading them would make it a second place that defines a rule. *Test:* `work_a_heed_is_given_its_settings`

**C-work-59** A heed's run is labelled with the request's team, never its rule's, and what it read is that team's; it takes no room among the runs at work. A rule Bulldog ships is of no team, and a burst of messages weighed must not hold back the leads' runs, nor wait behind them. *Test:* `work_a_heed_run_is_the_teams`, which holds that with `runs: 1` and the bot's run held, a heed weighs another request in #help and notes it, its run labelled `team=office`, and its entry what was read of the office.

**C-work-60** While a heed of a request goes, no lead starts on it, and no second heed: what is said meanwhile is weighed next, or given to the lead with the rest when the heed acts. A lead started beside its heed would answer what the heed may note, and two runs on one request would race. *Test:* `work_no_lead_while_a_heed_weighs`

**C-work-53** A request is never left working with nothing going, nothing waiting to start and nothing it waits on: whatever ends the last of these either starts its next run or answers it. A request shown as working with nothing to move it waits for ever, and nobody is told. *Test:* `work_no_request_working_with_nothing_to_move_it`

## What a run is given

**R-work-06** A run is started with its worker's definition as it is written, with one thing filled in: a model named by one word is replaced by the model of that name Office gives, from the worker's own team or else the nearest team around it; a model written out in full is sent as it is, and nothing else is filled in. The run has three inputs: `said`, what is new for this run as text; `context`, as text a model reads well; and `request`, the request as data.
*Accept:* the house's team file names `usual` among its `models:`, and the steward writes `model: usual`. The stored definition Corbie shows for the steward's run is the steward's file as written, with the house's `usual` written out in full in place of the word, and its tools file and its ask as the file gives them. Sparks writes its model out in full, and its run's model is as written. The run's input holds `said`, `context` and `request`, and a definition that names only `said` among its inputs runs.

**R-work-07** `context` holds the date and time in the office's zone; the team's name, its people with their titles and the workers who sit with the worker; the team's notes (R-work-24); the teams of the office it may ask, each with its `about`, the one line its team file gives for what it looks after; who asked, as a person of the office with the teams they are in, or as a guest with the identity the bridge vouched for; where, and who will read the answer; the thread so far, less what is new for the run (R-work-09), each message the team's heed noted marked `(noted)` after who it was said to, left out when nothing else is in it; then, under "New for you", each entry new for the run, with when it was said and who said it (C-work-55); and last, that the run's last reply is its answer, and, for a routine or at a channel place, but from a run started again on a refusal, that an empty reply says nothing. Who sits with it and the teams it may ask are read from the files when the run starts.
*Accept:* the stand-in records the prompt of the steward's first turn: it holds the date and time with the office's zone, "The house", Sam and Kit with their titles, the fixer, sparks, the clerk, bins and the builder, the garden with its `about`, Kit as the asker, in the house, in the built-in chat, the readers of the answer, Kit's words under "New for you" with their time and Kit's name and no thread above them, and last the line saying its last reply is said to Kit, with nothing of an empty reply. It names no worker of the garden. A guest asks payments in #refunds, a place that names payments, whose rule refuses every answer: the ledger's run started again on the refusal is told its last reply is said to the guest in #refunds, with nothing of an empty reply, and its empty reply tells the guest that payments did not disclose its answer. Someone the Slack bridge vouches for as `U0GUEST1`, who matches no person, asks in #help, a place whose settings let guests ask: the bot's context names a guest with that identity. The garden's `about` is changed to name the pond too, and the steward's next run names the pond. The work office's heed notes Dana's "Raj, I'll look at it" in #help, and she then says "@bot what do you think?": the bot's thread holds "Dana to Platform support (noted): Raj, I'll look at it", nothing of the heed's note, and under "New for you" only her second message.

**R-work-08** `request` holds the request's number, its asker as a person with their teams or as a guest with the identity the bridge vouched for, its origin, who reads its answer, its entries, each with who it is by and to, when, and `noted`, whether the team's heed noted it, less those new for the run, which it holds apart as `new`, whole and in the same form, its sub-requests, each with its team and where it stands, and its team's notes, each under its name.
*Accept:* a lead that is a flow whose output is its input `request` is run on a request that is then asked a sub-request, and again when something more is said in it. Its second answer, written out as text, holds the number, the asker, the origin, the readers, the earlier entries with their by, to and time, what was said more among `new` and not among the entries, the sub-request with its team and state, and its team's notes. Dana's noted message is given to the bot with `noted: true`, and her second with `noted: false`.

**R-work-09** The thread given to a run is its newest entries less those new for the run, and less a heed's words on what it noted (R-requests-47), which no run is given, up to 20,000 characters, with the opening entry always kept and a line saying how many entries are not shown. What is new for the run is given apart from it, whole, and counts nothing against the limit.
*Accept:* a request whose thread holds 40 entries of 1,000 characters each is given the opening entry, the newest entries that fit, and a line saying how many are left out; the opening entry is there and the total is under 21,000 characters. Kit's "last", said after, is under "New for you" and not in the thread. The bot's thread, after its heed noted Dana's message, holds the message and not the heed's entry by `for-us.js`.

**C-work-55** What is new for a run is given in `context` after the thread and apart from it, under "New for you", each entry on its own line with when it was said and who said it, whole however long, and only there; the thread is left out when nothing else is in it. One message given bare after a heading reads as part of the heading, the same words in the thread above say them twice, and a long message cut by the window loses what was asked. *Test:* `work_new_entries_shown_with_who_said_them`, which holds that a first run asked "hello" is given it as Kit's entry with its time under "New for you", with no thread, and that a message longer than the thread's limit is given whole, there and in `new`, and not in the thread.

**C-work-06** A sub-request's run is given its own thread and nothing of the request above it, and is told who asked it and the origin of the request at the top as who will read the answer. What was said in the asking team was said inside that team, and the team asked must still know where its answer may end up. *Test:* `work_sub_request_given_its_own_thread_only`

**C-work-07** What a run is told about where its answer goes is true for that run: the origin for a request, the asking worker for a sub-request, the team's people for a routine. A worker told the wrong thing about what its ending does acts on it. *Test:* `work_run_told_truly_where_its_answer_goes`

**C-work-08** Every time given to a run, in `context`, in `request` and in a tool's result, is in the office's zone and names it. Two clocks in one account of a request mislead a model about what came first. *Test:* `work_times_in_the_office_zone`

**C-work-09** A worker that is no model gets everything it is given as data in `request` as well as in text. A flow or a statechart cannot read words written for a model. *Test:* `work_worker_that_is_no_model_given_data`

**C-work-10** Text is never cut inside a character. A cut in the middle of one puts text that is not valid into the thread and into a model's input. *Test:* `work_text_never_cut_inside_a_character`

## How a run ends

**R-work-10** A run's answer is its output: text as it is, the text of `answer` when the output is data that has one, and other data written out as text.
*Accept:* three leads end with the text "done", with `{ answer: "done", spare: 1 }`, and with `{ n: 3 }`. The three answers are "done", "done", and the data `{ n: 3 }` written out.

**R-work-11** The answer is an entry in the thread, by the worker, to the asker, and the request is answered. It goes to the origin for a request, to the asking tool for a sub-request, and to the team's people for a routine.
*Accept:* Kit's request ends with an entry by the steward to Kit, its text the steward's answer, and the request answered; Bridges is handed it for the built-in chat. Payments' answer to the bot's sub-request is the bot's tool result. The house's Monday routine's answer is an entry to Sam and Kit.

**R-work-12** An empty answer to a routine, or to a request asked at a channel place, any place on a bridge but its direct place, says nothing: the request is answered with no entry, and the answer is handed to no disclosure. An empty answer to any other request, in the chat, in a direct message or a sub-request, is said to the asker as the team having given no answer. From a run started by a refused answer, an empty answer is the lead giving up (R-work-46) wherever it was asked.
*Accept:* the bins flow ends with an empty output on its routine: no entry goes to Sam or Kit, and the request is answered. The steward ends with an empty reply to Kit in the chat, and to Kit in a Telegram direct message: each time Kit is told the house gave no answer. In the work office, Dana says "thanks @bot" in #help and the bot replies with nothing: its context said an empty reply says nothing, the request is answered, and its thread holds only Dana's words. A guest in a place naming payments, whose disclosure check is a rule, is answered by the ledger with nothing: the request is answered, and no disclosure is asked.

**R-work-13** An answer leaving a team that set a disclosure check is handed to Decisions, and is said only once it is disclosed.
*Accept:* in the work office, the ledger ends its sub-request with an answer. Until the disclosure rule lets it go, the bot's tool still waits and no entry from payments is seen outside payments. Once it is disclosed, it is the bot's tool result.

**R-work-46** A refused answer comes back as an entry by the decider to the team's lead, with the decider's note, and starts the lead again on the same request with it as `said`; the restarts count among the runs in a row (C-work-04). The asker is told nothing of the refusal while the lead tries again, only what it next says that is disclosed. When the lead gives up, ending with no answer, or the refused answers use up the runs in a row, the asker is told the one line Decisions gives (R-decisions-40): that the team did not disclose its answer, and who refused it, with nothing of the answer or the note. The request is stopped by Bulldog with that line in place of its name (R-requests-21), so the asker is told nothing else.
*Accept:* the ledger's answer to the bot names an account number; the disclosure rule passes it, and Lee refuses with the note "the last four digits only". An entry by Lee to the ledger, payments' lead, with the note is in the sub-request's thread, seen only in payments, and a new run of the ledger starts on the sub-request with it as `said`; the bot's tool still waits and its result never holds the refusal. The ledger answers without the number, the rule lets it go, and it is the bot's tool result. Lee refuses again, and the ledger's next run ends with an empty reply: the bot's tool result is "payments did not disclose its answer (Lee)". With a rule `refuse-all.js` that refuses every answer, the ledger is started 20 times in a row, and the bot's tool result is "payments did not disclose its answer (refuse-all.js)", holding none of the ledger's answers and no note.

**R-work-14** A run that fails, runs out of time or of turns, or is refused a permission writes two entries by Bulldog: one to the team's people (R-requests-36), which is what was read of the team, holding what Corbie said and the line that would fix a refusal; and one to the asker, sent to the origin, saying that the team's work failed and it could not answer. With nothing else open in the request, that answers it; saying more in it makes it working again.
*Accept:* the house's lead is a flow whose one script reads a file it may not. Kit asks it something, and the run fails, denied. An entry by Bulldog to Sam and Kit, seen only in the house, holds what was refused, the layer that refused it and the fix line. The entry by Bulldog to Kit says the house's work failed and it could not answer, holds none of the error, and the request is answered. Kit replies, and the request is working again. Platform's worker fails on the bot's sub-request: Al has an entry by Bulldog holding what Corbie said, and the bot's tool result says platform could not answer. A lead whose run fails while a sub-request it made is still open leaves its request open, waiting on that sub-request.

**C-work-11** The asker of a run that failed is told that the team could not answer, and never the error. An error can hold what was read, such as a card number in a script's message, and what was read is seen only inside the team. *Test:* `work_asker_not_told_the_error`

**C-work-12** Nothing is tried again by itself after a run fails; a reply starts the request again. A run that failed once usually fails again, spending as it goes, and whether to try is a person's choice. *Test:* `work_failed_run_not_tried_again`

**R-work-15** A person who may stop a run stops it: Bulldog cancels it in Corbie, closes the questions it asked, stops the sub-requests it made, and the request is answered as stopped, saying by whom.
*Accept:* the bot's run waits on its sub-requests to platform and to payments, and in payments the ledger's refund is held, waiting on Lee. Al stops the bot's run. The bot's run, the platform worker's run, the ledger's run and its ask all end cancelled in Corbie; both sub-requests and the request are answered as stopped by Al; the question to Lee about the refund is closed.

**R-work-43** A request from an issue whose source reports it closed is stopped as a person's stop is, and Bulldog's entry says who closed it; no other change at the source starts or stops anything.
*Accept:* Dana's request comes from a GitLab issue, and the bot waits on its sub-request to platform. Dana closes the issue at GitLab, and the in machine reports it closed by her. The bot's run and the platform worker's run end cancelled, the sub-request is answered as stopped, and the request has Bulldog's entry saying Dana closed the issue. A label put on the issue instead writes no entry and starts no run.

**C-work-13** A stop is noted in the store as stopping before the cancel is sent to Corbie, the cancel is sent once, and the run is stopping until Corbie reports its end; a stop that cannot be noted is not sent, and the person is told. Without the mark, kept before the cancel goes, every look would send the cancel again. *Test:* `work_stop_sent_once`

**C-work-14** An ending is applied only to the run it is for, and only once: an ending from a run that is no longer its request's run is kept with that run and changes nothing else, and an ending seen by the watch and again by settling is applied once. Late and repeated endings must not answer a request a second time. *Test:* `work_ending_applied_once_to_its_own_run`

**R-work-16** Each run is kept with its request: the worker, when it started and ended, how it ended, and the tokens Corbie counted, for a run that failed as well.
*Accept:* after the steward answers and a second run fails at its turn limit, the request holds two runs, each with its worker, both times, how it ended and a token count above zero.

**C-work-15** An error in starting or ending a run, from Corbie or from the store, is always in the log and is said to the team's people (R-requests-36), and never dropped. Work that does not start without saying so is lost. *Test:* `work_start_errors_reported`

## Labels

**R-work-17** Every run Bulldog starts carries `bulldog` with the office's identity, which is never its name (C-server-17), and `kind`, `team`, and `request` on a run that has one, and is found by them.
*Accept:* a listing of the runs labelled with the house's identity holds every run Bulldog started for it: the steward's runs with `kind=work`, the routine's clocks with `kind=clock`, and the runs started for Bridges with the kind Bridges names. Each has `team`; each of the steward's runs has `request`, and no clock has.

**R-work-18** A run below a run Bulldog started carries the same labels, so a call from a colleague used as a tool is found with the lead's run.
*Accept:* the steward calls the fixer as a tool, and the fixer calls `ask_person`. The call's run is in the listing narrowed to the request's label, below the fixer's, below the steward's.

**C-work-17** Labels hold kinds, numbers and identities, never words of a request or anyone's name. Corbie shows labels to anyone who may read heads. *Test:* `work_labels_hold_no_words`

**R-work-47** A run started to decide belongs to the team it decides for: a rule's run and a worker's run, a worker of another team's included, carry that team's `team`, the `request` of the decision and the `kind` Decisions names, and what each read is seen only by that team's people. A rule is started as it is written, a bare script given the decision as input or a definition, in its team's folder or among those Bulldog ships, with no permission Bulldog grants and no definition Bulldog writes around it; a rule in its team's folder that is a definition has a model named by one word filled in as a worker's is (R-work-06), and one Bulldog ships is sent as it ships, with nothing filled in (R-office-35).
*Accept:* the garden writes `decide: { open_door: [reviewer, sam] }`, the reviewer being a worker of the house. The reviewer's run on the gardener's held `open_door` carries `team=garden` and the garden's request; Sam, the garden's person, may watch it, and Kit, who is in the house and not in the garden, may not. The house's `small-orders.js` runs as a bare script with no network: a call it makes to a host is refused. A rule written as a definition that allows the screening service's host reaches it, and its run's permissions are those its file holds and nothing more.

**C-work-18** Bulldog chooses a run's name and notes it, with the team and request it is for, before it sends the start, and what a run is for is read from that note and not from its labels. A start sent and then forgotten in a crash leaves a run nothing ends. *Test:* `work_run_noted_before_start`

## The tools Bulldog ships
